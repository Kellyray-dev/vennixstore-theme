# VennixStore Product-Image Alt-Text Guidelines

## Purpose

These guidelines define concise, accessible alt text for VennixStore product images. They are based on the current catalog, which includes women's and men's apparel, activewear, essentials, intimates, sets, and fashion accessories.

The current audit found 2,068 product images:

- 468 missing alt text
- 0 generic values under the audit rules
- 1,600 existing values classified as good

These guidelines are for future review and staged content preparation. They do not change Shopify data.

## Target format and length

Use this pattern where it accurately describes the image:

> `[Color] [style/fit] [productType], [detail visible in image]`

Target **5–10 words**. Use one short sentence or descriptive phrase. Do not force every part of the pattern when the image does not show or support it.

Examples:

- `Cream textured tie-front crop top and mini skirt set`
- `Floral sleeveless A-line mini dress`
- `Black reflective hooded windbreaker, front view`
- `Men's waffle-knit crew-neck T-shirt, textured fabric`

## 1. What good product-image alt text contains

Good alt text identifies the subject and the most useful visible distinction:

1. The visible product type, such as dress, polo shirt, jogger pants, bra, hat, or gloves.
2. A visible color or pattern when it helps distinguish the item.
3. A visible style or fit, such as cropped, wide-leg, sleeveless, hooded, or full-cup.
4. One meaningful visible detail, such as a graphic, texture, tie-front, reflective panel, or pocket.
5. The view when it changes what the shopper needs to understand, such as front view, back view, flat lay, or close-up.

Write for someone who cannot see the image. Describe the product, not the file name, marketing campaign, or search keywords.

## 2. Describe only what is actually visible

- Use the product title or catalog data as a starting point, then confirm the feature is visible in the specific image.
- Do not infer color from the product title if the image does not show it clearly.
- Do not claim a material, performance benefit, softness, fit, size, brand, or construction detail unless it is visible or explicitly verified for that image.
- Do not describe a person’s identity, body, attractiveness, or presumed gender.
- Do not guess the setting, occasion, or intended use from styling alone.
- If a detail is obscured, cropped out, or too small to verify, leave it out.
- For a product-only image, describe the product. For a model photo, describe the product and the visible pose or view only when useful.

## 3. Color, style, fit, and visible details

### Color

Use a plain color name shoppers can understand: `cream`, `black`, `floral`, `purple`, or `blue`. Mention a secondary color only when it is clearly visible and useful.

### Style and fit

Use style or fit terms supported by the image, such as `A-line`, `bodycon`, `slim-fit`, `wide-leg`, `loose-fit`, `cropped`, `crew-neck`, `turtleneck`, `hooded`, `sleeveless`, or `full-cup`.

Avoid converting a catalog claim into a visual claim. For example, use `slim-fit` only when the silhouette is apparent; do not use `comfortable`, `moisture-wicking`, or `supportive` as image descriptions.

### Visible details

Prefer concrete details:

- `floral print`
- `tie-front detail`
- `reflective panel`
- `waffle-knit texture`
- `ruffle neckline`
- `elastic waistband`
- `skull print`
- `half-finger design`

Use no more than one or two details so the result stays within the target length.

## 4. Handling common image views

### Front views

Describe the product and its front-facing visible features. Add `front view` only when it distinguishes the image from other product images.

Example: `Black polo shirt, front view with collar`

### Back views

Identify the product and describe visible back details such as a back graphic, hood, yoke, or pocket.

Example: `Vintage-wash T-shirt, back graphic view`

### Flat lays

Describe the item as arranged in the image. Do not imply that it is being worn.

Example: `Cream tie-front crop top, flat lay`

### Close-ups

Name the product first, then identify the visible close-up detail.

Example: `Waffle-knit T-shirt, textured fabric close-up`

### Model photos

Describe the garment or accessory being shown. Add `model wearing` only when the context helps distinguish the image, and do not describe the model’s identity or appearance.

Example: `Floral A-line mini dress on model`

## 5. Avoid keyword stuffing

- Write one natural description, not a list of search terms.
- Do not repeat the product title, color, or product type.
- Do not add the store name, vendor name, SKU, handle, or URL.
- Do not add sales language such as `best`, `sale`, `must-have`, or `shop now`.
- Do not add unrelated occasions or categories just to capture searches.
- Use the shortest wording that gives a shopper useful visual information.

## 6. Avoid generic descriptions

Never use `image`, `photo`, `pic`, `product`, `picture`, a file name, or an empty value as the complete alt text. These labels do not explain what is visible.

Bad:

- `image`
- `Photo`
- `pic`
- `product image`
- `IMG_1234`
- `Shop VennixStore clothing`

Better:

- `Black hooded windbreaker, reflective panel`
- `Floral sleeveless A-line mini dress`
- `Skull-print motorcycle balaclava`

## Review checklist

Before approving an alt value, confirm:

- It describes the visible product and not the file.
- It is usually 5–10 words.
- Color, style, fit, and details are visibly supported.
- The view is included when it matters.
- It contains no keyword stuffing or sales copy.
- It is not `image`, `photo`, `pic`, `product`, or a similar generic label.
- It is different from neighboring images when the visible view or detail differs.
